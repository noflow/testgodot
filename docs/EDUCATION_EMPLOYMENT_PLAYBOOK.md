# Education, Employment, and Activities Playbook

## Westshore College

Westshore offers seven starting programs: Arts and Humanities, Business, Computer
Systems, Education, Health Sciences, Social Sciences, and Undeclared Studies. The
first-semester catalog contains fifteen courses with fixed Morning, Lunch, or
Afternoon sections. The campus does not schedule classes after Afternoon.

Full-time enrollment normally contains four courses and costs $4,000 per semester.
Part-time enrollment normally contains two courses and provisionally costs $2,200.
Books and supplies average $450. Enrolled players receive a weekly family allowance
of $60, $100, or $150 according to the selected financial background.

Grades combine attendance, assignments, projects, and examinations. Energy, Focus,
Stress, Study skill, relevant subject skills, and preparation affect performance.
Classes and jobs may be double-booked, but the player cannot attend both.

## Employment

The opening catalog contains thirteen jobs covering retail, food service, cinema,
campus work, healthcare administration, fitness, transit, warehousing, office work,
and regulated adult self-employment. Hourly jobs meet the $18 minimum wage.

Every job defines:

- Full-time, part-time, or variable status
- Required skills, traits, education, attributes, and availability
- One or more weekly schedules
- Pay, tips where applicable, and promotion paths
- Optional character referrals

The regulated professional-companion route is adult-only, voluntary, licensed,
non-graphic, and governed by health, consent, safety, privacy, and refusal rules.

## Interviews and work performance

Interview scores combine requirements, answers, punctuality, presentation, traits,
reputation, referrals, and a clean weather-appropriate outfit. A score of 65 can
produce a standard offer; 82 can produce a strong offer. Missing hard requirements,
appearing intoxicated, or failing to attend prevents an offer.

Employment begins with a thirty-day probation period. Attendance, punctuality,
task quality, skills, Energy, Focus, and feedback affect performance. Raises are
reviewed approximately every ninety days, while promotions also require an opening.

## Repeatable activities

Twenty-seven activities currently cover hygiene, wardrobe, laundry, food, alcohol,
sleep, studying, job applications, interview practice, gym training, park exercise,
movies, texting, chores, therapy, doctor visits, and sexual-health screening.

Short actions may share an activity block until their combined duration reaches one
full block. Repeating activities far below the player's skill eventually stops
providing useful experience. Health, fatigue, injury, weather, and intoxication
checks can restrict unsafe activities.
